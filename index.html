Opening Chapter Excerpt

The Marriage Clause | Contemporary Billionaire Romance

Tension Level: High

Chapter 1

The invitation was heavy, gilded, and smelled offensively of bergamot and old money.

Julian Thorne did not invite people to dinner. He summoned them. And after three years of watching him aggressively outbid my firm for every boutique real estate developer on the East Coast, the last place I wanted to sit was across from his mahogany table.

"You look like you are preparing for a court trial, Nora," my assistant, Chloe, whispered as she smoothed the lapel of my tailored ivory blazer.

"Worse," I replied, checking my lipstick in the elevator glass. "I am preparing to smile at a snake for two hours without breaking a glass."

The elevator chimed, depositing me on the penthouse floor of The Grand Regent. The dining hall was dimly lit, designed with rich leather, warm brass, and floor-to-ceiling glass overlooking the Manhattan skyline.

And there he sat.

Julian Thorne was wearing a charcoal suit with the top button unfastened, his dark hair pushed back with effortless carelessness. He held a crystal glass of amber liquid, his gray eyes catching mine the second I stepped out of the foyer. He did not smile. He simply watched me approach, calculating every step as if estimating the market value of my stride.

"Miss Vance," he said, his voice a low, gravelly baritone that vibrated against the back of my neck. He stood up, towering over the table. "You are four minutes late."

"The traffic on Madison was atrocious, Mr. Thorne," I said, taking the seat across from him before his maître d' could even pull out the chair for me. "Though I am sure you could have bought the street and cleared it if it bothered you that much."

A faint, almost imperceptible twitch caught the corner of his mouth. "Punctuality is a sign of respect. But I suppose we both know why you are here, and respect has little to do with it."

"I am here because your board requested a joint merger presentation for the Sterling Account," I reminded him, leaning back and resting my hands on the white tablecloth. "Nothing more."

Julian leaned forward, setting his glass down with a quiet, deliberate tap. "The board requested a presentation. But I requested you."

My pulse hiccuped. I forced my expression to remain flat. "Excuse me?"

"My father's trust clause activates in thirty days," Julian said, his gaze locking onto mine with alarming intensity. "He specified that the controlling shares of Thorne Global will only transfer to me if I am legally engaged to a long-term strategic partner. Someone respected in the industry. Someone immune to public scandal."

I let out a dry, incredulous laugh. "And you think I am going to write your press release?"

"No," Julian said softly, his gray eyes cutting straight through me. "I want you to marry me."

Bound by Blood | Paranormal Shifter Romance

Tension Level: Peak Cliffhanger

Chapter 14 (Excerpt Ending)

The forest was silent, save for the crunch of pine needles beneath my boots.

I clutched the silver vial against my ribs, the cool metal burning through the fabric of my jacket. Three miles to the border. If I could cross the river before the moon reached its peak, the Silverwood Pack would never track the scent of the stolen cure.

A low, guttural growl vibrated through the earth beneath my feet.

I froze.

The shadows between the ancient oaks shifted. Out stepped an enormous jet-black wolf, his shoulders broader than an iron gate, his golden eyes glowing in the moonlight. Blood dripped from his left flank, but his stance was steady, lethal, and unmistakable.

Devren.

My breath hitched in my throat. He was not supposed to be here. He was supposed to be leading the perimeter patrol five miles north.

The wolf shifted. The horrific sound of bones cracking and snapping filled the quiet glade as he took human form. Devren stood before me in the moonlight, chest heaving, bare skin covered in fresh mud and crimson streaks.

"You took it," Devren rasped, his voice raw from the shift. He took a heavy step toward me, his golden eyes blazing with a dangerous mix of betrayal and sheer primal fury.

"Devren, please," I whispered, stepping back until my spine hit the cold bark of a hemlock tree. "You don't understand what they're going to do to him. I had to."

"You lied to me," he growled, closing the distance between us in a single terrifying stride. He pinned his hand against the tree right beside my ear, trapping me in his heat. His scent of cedar, rain, and ozone flooded my senses, making my wolf howl inside my chest. "Every kiss. Every night in my bed. Was all of it just a distraction?"

"No," I choked out, tears stinging the backs of my eyes. "Devren, I love you."

"Then drop the vial," he ordered, his eyes flashing wolf-gold.

My fingers tightened around the silver cylinder. I looked into the eyes of the man I loved, and then at the river roaring just fifty yards behind him.

"I'm sorry," I whispered.

Before he could react, I smashed the glass smoke-pellet at my feet. A blinding white cloud erupted between us. Devren roared in fury as I turned and sprinted toward the cliff edge, diving headfirst into the freezing black rapids below.
